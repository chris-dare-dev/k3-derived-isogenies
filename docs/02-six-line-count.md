# 2. Six-line surfaces against Hermitian lattices: the count

*Counts six-line double planes with all lines rational against the unimodular Hermitian lattices predicted by the dictionary of [note 1](01-prior-art.md): the computation of note 1 §5, items 2–4. Labels: **[P]** read in the cited source; **[M]** recalled, not checked; **[D]** derived here; **[E]** measured by the scripts in `experiments/`.*

---

## 1. Result

For every prime 7 ≤ p ≤ 37, all double planes w² = ℓ₁⋯ℓ₆ branched along six F_p-rational lines in general position were enumerated up to isomorphism, with their Frobenius polynomials. For each ordinary polynomial that is irreducible of degree 6, the number of K3 surfaces was compared with the number of unimodular Hermitian lattices that note 1's dictionary predicts.

| p | classes of configurations | irreducible ordinary polynomials | lattice count computed | agree | K3 surfaces in agreeing cases |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 7, 11 | 3, 15 | 0 | – | – | – |
| 13 | 26 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 |
| 17 | 74 | 2 | 2 | 2 | 3 |
| 19 | 117 | 2 | 2 | 2 | 4 |
| 23 | 257 | 4 | 4 | 4 | 14 |
| 29 | 682 | 9 | 9 | 9 | 45 |
| 31 | 905 | 10 | 10 | 10 | 58 |
| 37 | 1,910 | 16 | 16 | 16 | 153 |
| **total** | **3,989** | **44** | **44** | **44** | **278** |

**The two counts agree in all 44 cases, with no mismatch.** [E] Forty polynomials (235 surfaces) were computed with the lattice search of this note. Of these, 24 have maximal endomorphism order and 16 do not; in 4 of the 16 there is no lattice over the maximal order at all, and every surface comes from a smaller order. Counts per polynomial range from 1 to 26. The 4 remaining polynomials (p = 29: one; p = 37: three) were too slow for this search, because of their discriminant groups (orders 2,401 to 28,561); with the faster lattice search of [note 5](05-non-split-count.md) §6 they have 7, 7, 19 and 10 surfaces, each equal to the lattice count, and that search reproduces all 40 other rows unchanged. The table gives the complete figures.

This is the worked example and the count of note 1 §1, in a family where everything is computable. It also tests the three derived statements of note 1 §4 that Statement 1 of note 1 depends on.

## 2. Setting [D]

**The family.** X: w² = ℓ₁⋯ℓ₆, resolved at its 15 nodes. N ⊂ NS(X̄) is the rank-16 lattice spanned by the 15 exceptional curves, the hyperplane class h and the six line classes; disc N = −2⁶. If all six lines are rational, Frobenius acts on N by p. The quadratic twist w² = c·ℓ₁⋯ℓ₆ keeps that and replaces F by −F on N^⊥.

**The rank-6 part.** T₆ = N^⊥ has discriminant group (Z/2)⁶, so T₆^∨ = ½T₆ and T₆ = L(2) with L unimodular of signature (2, 4). Such an L is odd and indefinite, hence L ≅ I_{2,4}: the Z-genus is automatic. Gluing N and T₆ into a unimodular F-stable lattice forces F to act trivially on T₆^∨/T₆, that is

> **(F − 1)/2 ∈ End(L).**

Because rank N = 16 ≥ ℓ(A_N) + 2, the map O(N) → O(q_N) is onto (Nikulin), so each isometry class of (L, F) glues to exactly one Taelman triple.

**The order.** End(L) must contain R_min, generated by F, p²/F, (F − 1)/2 and, at p, the unit F/p on the slope-1 part (condition (M4)).

**The prediction.** Let P be ordinary and irreducible, E = Q[t]/P, E₀ its real cubic subfield, τ a real place of E₀. Then

> #{K3 surfaces over F_p in the family with Frobenius polynomial P on T₆} = N_total(τ),

where N_total(τ) is the number of classes of pairs (L, α): L ⊂ E a lattice stable under R_min, α ∈ E₀, L unimodular for Tr(α x ȳ), α positive at τ and negative at the other two places, modulo (L, α) ∼ (γL, α/γγ̄). The number must not depend on τ.

**Counting N_total.** A unimodular R_min-lattice L sits between K = (L : O_E) and J = O_E·L, with K the α-dual of J. So L is the same thing as an integral Hermitian O_E-lattice (K, α) with αKK̄𝔡_E = 𝔞 for a conjugation-stable ideal 𝔞 dividing the conductor of R_min, together with an R_min-stable self-dual subgroup of K^#/K that generates it over O_E, taken up to the roots of unity of E. For 𝔞 = O_E this is the maximal-order term N(τ), which is Bayer-Fluckiger's C⁺(E) count (note 1).

**Counting surfaces.** A K3 surface in the family has two six-line polarisations up to automorphism, h and the one giving the associated (Gale dual) configuration, because O(q_N) ≅ S₆ × Z/2 [M: Matsumoto–Sasaki–Yoshida] and the stabiliser of h is S₆. In coordinates where the lines are the frame and (1, b, c), (1, e, f), association is (b, c, e, f) ↦ (b, e, c, f). So

> #K3 = (self-associated classes) + (other classes)/2,

counted over the classes of configurations whose polynomial is P(t) or P(−t).

## 3. What was measured [E]

1. **Enumeration.** All (b, c, e, f) in general position, reduced modulo the 720 relabellings; S_k = Σ χ(ℓ₁⋯ℓ₆) over P²(F_{p^k}) for k = 1, 2, 3, which is the trace of F^k on T₆. The fast sums were checked against direct point counts.
2. **(F − 1)/2 is integral in all 3,989 classes.** The functional equation leaves the sign of det F open when e₃ = 0. In every class exactly one sign gives a Weil polynomial with P(1 + 2s) ≡ 0 mod 64. So the 2-adic prediction holds everywhere and also fixes the sign, without point counts over F_{p⁴}.
3. **Association preserves the zeta function up to the twist**, in every class.
4. **Self-association marks higher Picard rank.** A self-associated class has reducible polynomial (ρ̄ ≥ 18), except in the 22 classes whose field E contains Q(i). There the extra root of unity is an automorphism exchanging the two polarisations, as §2 predicts. Conversely every polynomial with a fourth root of unity in E has self-associated classes.
5. **N(τ) and N_total(τ) are the same for the three real places** in all 44 and 40 cases.
6. **#K3 = N_total(τ)** in all 40 cases computed here, and in the 4 of note 5 §6.

Examples (p; e₁, e₂, e₃; classes with P(t), P(−t); #K3; N; N_total; class number of E; index of R_min):

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 23 | (2, 207, −2116) | 5, 7 | 6 | 6 | 6 | 12 | 1 |
| 37 | (10, −185, −27380) | 21, 31 | 26 | 26 | 26 | 52 | 1 |
| 37 | (18, −185, −49284) | 11, 5 | 8 | 8 | 8 | 64 | 1 |

## 4. What this confirms, and one correction to a source

- **Note 1 §4.2 and §4.3.** The torsor is over the Hermitian lattices with (NS, F|NS) fixed, and one triple per lattice. A count by "same zeta function" alone would be off by the twists and by the second polarisation.
- **Non-maximal orders (note 1 §1, item 1).** The count over all orders containing R_min is right, and R_min is the right minimal order: a smaller order would overcount, a larger one would undercount in the 16 non-maximal cases.
- **Taelman's Theorem B needs a positivity condition.** [D] Conditions (M1)–(M5), as stated in arXiv v2, do not say which real place of E₀ carries the positive-definite plane of T_R. For a fixed embedding ι, the lattice of a surface is positive on the plane of the unit-root eigenvalue. Triples that are positive at another place satisfy (M1)–(M5) but are not H² of a K3 surface for that ι, since M^{2,0} ⊕ M^{0,2} would be negative definite. The essential image is the triples positive at τ_ι. The data decide this: the surfaces number N_total(τ) for one τ, not the sum over the three places. I have not checked whether the published version states the condition.
- **The lattice count is independent of τ**, as it must be if each choice of ι gives a bijection with the same set of surfaces. This is a check on the lattice code that uses no geometry.

## 5. Limits

- **Derived statements.** §2 is my derivation. Three geometric steps are assumed, not proved here: that a K3 surface over F_p with NS(X̄) ≅ N and F = p on it is a six-line double plane with rational lines; that N is primitive in H² for every member; and the group O(q_N) ≅ S₆ × Z/2 with association as the Z/2. The agreement in 44 cases supports all three but does not prove them.
- **Small numbers at small p.** Below p = 29 most classes lie on loci of higher Picard rank, so only 9 polynomials come from p ≤ 23.
- **Four cases not computed by this note's search** (p = 29: one; p = 37: three). All have Q(i) ⊂ E and conductors of norm 2,401 to 28,561. The subgroup enumeration here is a plain search; using the Hermitian property to test isotropy on R-generators only would make them feasible, and the faster search of note 5 §6 computes them: they agree.
- **Only split forms.** Configurations whose lines are permuted by Frobenius are not enumerated. They have a different F on NS and belong to other torsors.
- **No derived equivalence is exhibited.** The count shows that the surfaces with a given polynomial are as many as the lattices. It does not identify which surface is which lattice, and it realises no ℓ-neighbour step by an explicit twisted Fourier–Mukai partner. Note 1 §4.4 stands: ℓ = 2 is the bad prime here.

## 6. Next steps

1. **Label the torsor.** For one polynomial with several surfaces (say p = 23, six surfaces, h(E) = 12), decide which surfaces are ℓ-neighbours. The Elkies criterion of note 1 §3.2 says which ℓ give steps; an ℓ-step corresponds to an F-stable isotropic line in Br(X̄)[ℓ]. For ℓ = 3 this might be made explicit through the 3-torsion of a genus-2 or elliptic fibration on these surfaces.
2. **Non-split forms**, to see the dependence on F|NS.
3. **A Honda–Tate count**: enumerate all admissible polynomials at a given p and check that Σ N_total equals the number of ordinary surfaces with ρ̄ = 16, including polynomials for which no surface was found.
4. **Write the assembly note**: the assembly proposition of note 1 §5, the R_min description of orders, the positivity remark, and this table.

Item 1 is done at p in [notes 3](03-torsor-action.md) and [4](04-forms-over-the-prime-field.md), item 2 for involutions in [note 5](05-non-split-count.md), and item 4 is [note 6](06-torsors-under-cm-class-groups.md). Item 3 is done for the seven primes of the table in [note 13](13-hardness-on-paper.md) §3.2, for the polynomials of the shape that the Artin–Tate formula allows: the admissible polynomials there are exactly the 44 polynomials of the enumerated surfaces, so none is without a surface.

## Reproduce

From `experiments/`, with `python -I`:

```bash
python -I sixlines.py selftest
```

```bash
for p in 7 11 13 17 19 23 29 31 37; do python -I sixlines.py enumerate --p $p --out sixlines_p$p.jsonl --procs 8; done
```

```bash
python -I sixlines_cm.py --list --out sixlines_cm.jsonl sixlines_p{7,11,13,17,19,23,29,31,37}.jsonl > sixlines_cm.log
```

The enumeration takes about 12 minutes in total (p = 37 alone about 7); the comparison under one minute. `sixlines_cm.py` needs PARI through `cypari2`. It uses the faster lattice search of [note 5](05-non-split-count.md) §6, which also computes the four polynomials that the original search (still available with `--slow`) leaves open. The sign test `sl_mask` in `sixlines_cm.py` raises the precision as needed; with the 15-digit arithmetic it once used it could fail ([note 5](05-non-split-count.md) §5), but the output of the command above, `sixlines_cm.jsonl` and its log, was byte-identical after the fix.
